# Training-only feasibility inventory for continuation labels

The [completed development continuation diagnostic](consequence_fixed_base_continuation_results_20260928.md) shows why an immediate-clear disagreement cannot automatically be treated as a terminal-value error. The next preparatory step inventories existing training actions before collecting any new continuation label. It does not fit a model, alter an acceptance gate, or admit the rejected scorer.

Read the admitted original and expanded action labels for all 47,149 play states from training seeds 4,000,000 through 4,000,999. Match identities, factual snapshot hashes, UID counters, teacher outcomes and resources; reconcile the existing 245,637 original and 47,125 added labels. Exclude states where base chooses a non-play action from this play-only feasibility population.

Choose three distinct existing labelled plays per eligible state. In order, retain the teacher action, base action and the already measured added action, deduplicating action identities. If fewer than three distinct actions have been selected, fill from remaining original proposals in ascending global action ID. This preserves teacher/base alternatives and includes an assessed alternative beyond them even when teacher and base agree. No new preference forward or immediate-score maximization is used. Exclude states with fewer than three available actions, and separately count cases where all three actions already terminate the game. The continuation pool requires at least one nonterminal branch.

Report pool sizes and immediate-outcome diversity in four groups: early/later (Ante below 4 versus at least 4), crossed with base matching/differing from the teacher action. Count complete source-game coverage and distinct actions. These are existing immediate labels; their diversity does not establish diversity or learnability of terminal continuation outcomes.

Select an eight-state runtime pilot before observing any new continuation outcome: two states from each group, with eight distinct source games. Restrict pilot eligibility to the first 64 training seeds to bound factual-prefix reconstruction. Within each group, order by SHA256 of the ASCII string `continuation-training-pilot-v1:{seed}:{step}`, then seed and step. Traverse groups lexicographically and take the first two rows whose source games have not already been used. Fail if any group cannot supply two cases; do not silently change the selection. Save all three actions with their original labels and snapshot identities. These eight states are a feasibility sample, not a performance estimate or a frozen full training population.

Use a standard-library inventory with a 600-second cap plus 30 seconds supervision grace. Recount the emitted eligible-state list, verify the pilot is a subset with the required action/group/game counts, and freeze source pins and selection code. No engine/model import, simulator call, hidden-state feature, development training label, new fit, new policy, confirmation access or full-collection commitment occurs. A later native pilot must separately freeze its common continuation controller, RNG/state restoration, trace audit and runtime limits before execution. The existing prediction, retention and final long-horizon requirements remain unchanged.
