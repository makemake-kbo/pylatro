# Immediate-clear deficits mostly disappear when the same blind is continued

The fixed-base scorer's 74 clear-losing overrides are not 74 failed blinds. After each selected action, the unchanged base controller eventually clears **73 of those 74 blinds**. Its paired base-action branches clear all 74. Terminal wins are **44 versus 41**, with an unresolved selected-minus-base difference of **+4.05 percentage points [−2.86, +11.29]**. This weakens an interpretation of the immediate-clear deficit as evidence of worse eventual play. It does not demonstrate that the scorer learned resource preservation or that its autonomous policy improves wins.

The [declared continuation diagnostic](consequence_fixed_base_continuation_20260928.md) covers all 136 overrides where base and the selected action disagree on immediate clearing: 74 lost clears and 62 gained clears, from 76 previously exposed development games. Each branch starts from the original teacher-path snapshot, takes its assigned action once, and then follows the same immutable, greedy base controller through termination. The candidate scorer makes no subsequent decisions. Four pilot cases are reused; the panel measures and independently audits the other 132.

Across all 136 paired interventions, both branches win in 63 cases and both lose in 46. The selected action produces a realized win where base loses in 16 cases; the opposite happens in 11. There is **no censoring** in either branch. The 79/136 and 74/136 counts are conditional suffix outcomes on selected states, not win rates for two autonomous policies starting fresh games.

The distinction between clearing now and completing the blind is substantial in both directions. Among the 62 immediate-clear gains, base still eventually clears 56 blinds, versus all 62 for the selected action. Among the 74 immediate-clear losses, the selected action still clears 73, versus all 74 for base. Combined eventual current-blind completions are therefore **135 selected versus 130 base**, despite the selected actions having 12 fewer immediate clears. Later shop choices, deck changes, growth, and different consumption of the same initial RNG stream can all affect terminal outcomes; these counts do not identify which mechanism caused an individual difference.

The preceding [head-alignment diagnostic](consequence_fixed_base_head_alignment_results_20260928.md) found that the clear head correctly preferred base in 52 of the 74 clear-losing cases. Both action branches eventually clear the blind in **all 52**. Selected terminal wins are 31 versus base's 30; three cases become wins and two become losses, with difference **+1.92 points [−6.67, +10.64]**. Thus correct prediction of the immediate clear does not make the corresponding preference disagreement an established long-horizon error. In the other 22 cases, terminal wins are 13 versus 11, difference **+9.09 points [0.00, +22.22]**; this interval does not have a strictly positive lower bound. None of the eleven reported descriptive group intervals establishes a positive or negative difference with both endpoints strictly on one side of zero.

The [saved-result analysis](consequence_fixed_base_continuation_analysis_20260928.md) reports the four gain/loss by early/later cells as well. It independently matches every case's action identity, observation hash, immediate outcome and grouping to the head-alignment records, recounts every native-audit summary, and verifies the saved per-case classifications. Intervals use 10,000 whole-source-game bootstrap draws, seed 1729, retaining all qualifying states within each sampled game and reporting state-balanced differences. These are unadjusted exploratory intervals on reused development data. They do not include independent-fit uncertainty or average over alternative hidden RNG states. With zero censoring, the whole-group missing-outcome bounds equal the observed differences; those bounds are not confidence intervals.

This result narrows the next mechanism test toward **continuation supervision**, while keeping representation and readout capacity controlled. Immediate score and clear labels teach immediate consequences, and teacher preferences do not explicitly label the eventual value of the alternative. Before another fit, establish a bounded, training-only collection of paired continuation outcomes that includes alternatives beyond the teacher/base pair, measures how often terminal outcomes actually distinguish actions, and profiles cost. Sampling, controller, budgets and any later fitting criteria must be specified before collection. Development interventions from this diagnostic must not become training labels. A later improvement from continuation targets would test supervision; it would not by itself support a larger encoder or independently learned features. No new fit or numerical experiment protocol is selected in this report.

The existing fixed-base endpoint remains rejected under its unchanged, predeclared clear-retention screen. This diagnostic neither overrides that decision nor validates the screen as a proxy for long-horizon performance. It also leaves the other 711 base overrides unmeasured at this horizon and does not test the interactions from repeatedly following the candidate. The original fresh sampled-win gain, greedy retention, early-policy attribution and final independent-fit confirmation requirements remain unmet. No world-model work, policy integration, fresh autonomous evaluation or confirmation fit follows from these results.
