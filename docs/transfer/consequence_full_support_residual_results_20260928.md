# Remaining scorer errors involve both consequence prediction and preference selection

After full-support preference training and the [saved-duration check](consequence_full_support_duration_results_20260928.md), most remaining net lost clears still come from selected actions outside the original proposal sets. The consequence outputs recognize some damaging corrections, but they also misorder important alternatives. Neither a better average prediction score nor simply following the clear head establishes safe action selection.

At the selected epoch 10, **123 of 126 net lost clears** lie in the outside group. Its selected-action log-score MSE is **2.170650**, versus **0.988667** averaged over the original proposals at those same states. Clear BCE is **0.535816** versus **0.347269**. Mean predicted clear probability is **45.74%**, while those selected actions clear in **29.75%** of recorded continuations. At epoch 20, outside selected MSE is **1.789396** versus proposal MSE **0.853154**; BCE is **0.436517** versus **0.344261**, and predicted/realized clear rates are **35.52% / 26.28%**.

These are selection-conditioned development groups, not a controlled subgroup comparison. “Outside” means outside the up-to-six proposals at that development state, not that the structural hand was never seen in training. The training preference loss now covers every legal action, while score/clear supervision still uses the original proposal-generation distribution. Correcting the preference denominator did not expand consequence labels. The selected-action errors expose a remaining distribution mismatch that the earlier proposal-only prediction screen did not capture.

Preference selection also sometimes rejects a better-ranked consequence prediction. Of epoch 10's **219 lost clears**, the chosen action has lower predicted clear probability than base in **110**, and lower predicted log score in **95**. Among its **93 gained clears**, those counts are **9** and **13**. Epoch 20 has lower predicted clear probability on **134 of 260** lost clears, and on **14 of 89** gained clears. These counts describe existing choices; no threshold, combined policy or new gate was selected from them. A lower immediate prediction can sometimes reflect a justified resource tradeoff, so these disagreements alone do not prove a wrong long-term policy.

The last-hand failures are more restrictive. Epoch 10 introduces **four immediate losses where base survives**; all four are last-hand states, all have base equal to teacher, and two select actions inside the original proposals. The clear predictor ranks base higher in only **one of the four**, and the score predictor does so in only **one** (a different case). Thus a simple preference-versus-clear consistency rule cannot explain away most of these fatal comparisons. Epoch 20 introduces three losses versus surviving base; its clear predictor favors base in two, and its score predictor in one. These counts differ from the nine/eight losses where the teacher survives, because base itself fails on some teacher-surviving states.

For a concrete persistent failure, at development seed 850000064, step 138, base clears with **13,296** points. Epoch 10 chooses a **5,002**-point fatal action while predicting both higher log score and higher clear probability for it than for base. Epoch 20 chooses a different **6,313**-point fatal action despite predicting lower score and clear probability than base. The same state therefore illustrates both consequence misranking and preference/consequence disagreement across saved durations. All labels are original-RNG realizations; the diagnostic does not estimate a conditional stochastic expectation or reveal hidden RNG to a predictor.

The inherited teacher-containing restricted-proposal calculation still fails overall retention: 2,671 clears at epoch 10 and 2,681 at epoch 20, versus base 2,699. Its action set includes teacher answers by construction and is not deployable. The inside-proposal fatal cases also show that proposal restriction alone would not solve every failure.

The next focused question is whether broader **training-only action-consequence coverage** improves these selected-action errors while retaining existing clears. First measure the saved scorer's training choices and how often they lie outside existing labels, to establish a concrete additional-label rule and replay cost before another fit. Keep features, architecture and full-support preference loss fixed if that label-coverage intervention proceeds. Independent feature learning remains an option after this gap is tested; longer context and world models have no new support from these results. The full long-horizon win-rate and final independent-confirmation requirements remain unmet.
