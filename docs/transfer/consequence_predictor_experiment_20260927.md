## One fit per readout

The benchmarked pooled MLP combines state, separate square-root-count-normalized selected/retained card sums and two counts; its two hidden layers have width 128. It has **131,971 trainable parameters** and three outputs: log-score, immediate-clear logit, and teacher preference. Base parameters stay frozen.

The matched relational prototype projects the same proposal representation to 64 dimensions, uses one four-head cross-attention readout over the 23 ordered context tokens (384-dimensional keys/values), and feeds the concatenated query/readout through a 128-wide layer to the same three outputs. Its natively verified capacity is **132,035 parameters**, 64 more than pooled (about 0.05%). Four technical checks pass for capacity, masking, gradients and proposal grouping; no optimizer ran. No independently trainable encoder or additional raw-state feature pathway is introduced in this comparison.

Train pooled first, then relational once each with the same fixed schedule: 20 complete shuffled passes over training play states, batch 128 states, AdamW at 0.001, betas (0.9, 0.999), epsilon 1e-8, weight decay zero, global gradient norm cap 1, random seed 0, no dropout. Use FP32 CPU with one Torch thread for the cached-feature heads. Fixed endpoint at pass 20; save passes 0, 5, 10 and 20 for diagnosis without selecting a different endpoint after evaluation. No hyperparameter sweep or second replica. Training-only normalization and one shared row-order manifest must be saved before either fit.

Use unit coefficients for standardized log-score MSE, clear BCE and teacher-preference cross-entropy. Average each consequence loss over valid proposals within a state, then over states, so states with more proposals do not get more weight. Standardize log-score with the training distribution's corresponding state-balanced mean and standard deviation; report errors again in original log-score units. Preference CE uses the teacher action among the distinct proposals. It expresses imitation, not a claim that maximizing score is optimal. Invalid padded proposals never contribute to any loss.

## Prediction and retention are separate gates

Report training and development log-score MSE, clear BCE/Brier score and preference NLL. Include both training-only constant baselines and training-only empirical means conditioned on selected-card count and preaction Ante bucket (1–3, 4–6, 7+), with an unconditional fallback for empty cells. Compute their means with the same equal-state/equal-within-state proposal weighting; clamp baseline clear probabilities to [1e-6, 1−1e-6] for finite BCE. Those aggregate baselines do not use simulator results from development. Use state-balanced losses and whole-game paired bootstrap intervals (10,000 draws, seed 1729). Report early and later strata, all proposals and non-teacher/non-base alternatives. Random-API strata from the pilot are diagnostic, not a complete determinism classification.

Prediction passes only when both development log-score MSE and clear BCE improve over both fixed aggregate baselines with paired 95% upper bounds below zero. Also report the relational-minus-pooled contrasts with identical rows and labels; a better raw metric alone is insufficient to attribute a meaningful architectural benefit.

After each fixed endpoint, assess its preference head across the complete legal play support on the same development teacher states. This is an offline candidate-choice diagnostic, not integration into autonomous policy behavior. Retain the base action unchanged when the base selects a non-play action; report those states separately. Replay every newly selected action, including any third action absent from the training proposal set, before counting clears or losses. Compare with the exact archived base outcomes on the same eligible states. The retention gate requires strictly more overall immediate clears, at least as many early clears, and no increase in either total immediate losses or newly introduced losses where the teacher survives. This clarification is declared before any fit or prediction results. Report gained/lost sets and whole-game intervals rather than hiding regressions in an average.
