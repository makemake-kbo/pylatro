# Terminal prediction errors include action differences, not only state confidence

The [saved-data decomposition](consequence_terminal_information_20260928.md) shows that the rejected RMS readout has both shared-state and action-relative validation error. Removing the common state-confidence contribution from the accounting does not remove the action error. The current evidence does not support fixing only state calibration or attributing failure simply to unseen coarse action shapes.

Relative to the fixed ante baseline, validation Brier excess **0.031628** splits exactly into **0.013793** from the three-action mean prediction and **0.017835** from centered action differences. The action component's 95% game-bootstrap interval is **[0.010450, 0.025506]**, above zero. BCE excess **0.134073** splits into shared-logit excess **0.086775** and action-dispersion excess **0.047299 [0.019431, 0.074929]**. The intervals for shared-state excess alone cross zero. These are exact loss decompositions of unchanged predictions, not alternative predictors or deployable state-only policies.

Action-relative probability variation contributes **0.021984** Brier cost in validation, while alignment with the realized within-state label differences recovers only **0.004149**. The corresponding training quantities are **0.016063** and **0.011015**. Thus action dispersion costs more than it recovers even in aggregate training Brier accounting, despite improved training pair ordering. Pair ranking and probability quality answer different questions.

Conditioning on each state's number of winning branches removes a common logit shift entirely. On the 166 mixed training states, conditional terminal-label NLL is **0.955715**, below uniform **log(3) = 1.098612**. On the 42 mixed validation states it is **1.355129**, above uniform by **0.256516 [−0.049947, 0.634369]**. That validation difference is unresolved, but does not demonstrate useful relative prediction. This proper conditional diagnostic is distinct from terminal-win calibration; it does not estimate expected action values. Base policy and existing preference scores have much larger conditional losses, but their scales were trained for action selection, so those comparisons do not establish superiority over calibrated terminal predictors.

At readout initialization, the BCE gradient's state-mean component has norm **1.02430**, while its within-state action component has norm **0.04147**, about **4.05%** as large. At the converged endpoint the two norms are **0.05263** and **0.05136**, with cosine **−0.99733**; their sum balances the fixed ridge penalty. These identities quantify the shared readout's fitting tradeoff. Gradient cancellation at an optimum is not by itself proof of harmful objective conflict or a reason to claim a new architecture will succeed.

The input representation has comparable action variation across splits: **17.61%** of normalized feature variance is within-state in training and **17.91%** in validation. Within-state label variance is **18.45% / 18.90%**. Learned-logit variance is **15.52% / 19.87%** within-state. This does not show a collapse of validation action features under these aggregate measures; it also cannot establish that the varying features encode the relevant terminal consequences.

All **84 validation terminal win/loss pairs** have training peers in outcome-independent coarse bins defined by ante bucket, last-hand indicator, selected-card counts and slot overlap. Only **three** lack a training terminal contrast in their bin. The median validation pair has **51** training source-game peers and **6.5** peers containing a terminal contrast. These bins omit ranks, suits, Jokers, retained resources and history, so coverage here cannot rule out missing consequential contexts. Uniform-outcome training states still contain information; absence of a realized win/loss contrast is not absence of an expected-value difference.

This supports one focused test before larger collection: train an action-relative conditional readout to remove shared-state outcome fitting from the objective, keeping the same frozen features and split. If relative fitting improves but validation remains weak, the broad state-prediction objective is insufficient to explain the transfer gap. If relative validation improves, it motivates separate source-game evaluation before any policy use. This is a discriminating next test, not an adopted policy or a revision of earlier gates. More capacity, more labels and altered regularization remain unproven alternatives.

The analysis reuses admitted arrays, reconstructs every output, exactly reconciles both loss decompositions and prior aggregate losses, and checks conditional likelihood by independently enumerating all winning subsets. No fit, simulator, policy or gate change occurs. The owner exits successfully. All data is exposed development evidence, and every source game contributed to the frozen feature fit; unseen-game generalization and fresh win-rate gains remain unproven.
