# Terminal-label sensitivity to hidden RNG interventions

The completed density experiment learns its enlarged training panel but fails validation. Before another fit, test whether recorded terminal preferences remain stable when hidden future randomness changes at an identical policy-visible decision. The existing RNG audit established input separation and immediate-outcome variation; this extension measures full-continuation label and pair-order sensitivity. It is a diagnostic, not a new training recipe.

Use only original terminal-label training decisions. Take the first 128 of the fixed 800 training source games in numeric source order. Stratify their original decisions by early/later ante (1–3 versus 4+) and original number of winning proposals (0, 1, 2 or 3). Select four decisions per stratum, minimizing SHA256 of ASCII `terminal-rng-stress-selection-v1:<seed>:<step>`, then source identity to break ties. This gives 32 distinct games and decisions, half with mixed original labels and half with homogeneous labels. The 128-game pool has at least four members per stratum, checked before any new intervention. This balanced prefix sample is not representative of the full corpus. Do not select using candidate errors or future intervention outcomes.

For each selected state, evaluate all three original proposals under four additional RNG initializations. Draw indices are 0–3. The fresh native `PseudorandomState` receives the SHA256 hexadecimal digest of `terminal-rng-stress-v1|<original observation hash>|<draw index>`, with empty channel state. The same fresh initialization is used across all three proposals of a state/draw, preserving normal native deep-copy coupling within action execution. This is the same intervention mechanism as the earlier input audit, with a separate declared seed domain. Keep the observed hand, ordered draw pile, Joker order, resources, history, already generated objects and all other runner state fixed. Do not shuffle observed cards, resample individual scoring probes, or expose seeds/RNG/labels to the policy.

This distribution is **not** the posterior over hidden RNG conditional on a naturally reached observation. It can establish sensitivity to this intervention and observation aliasing in the intervened simulator, but cannot estimate calibrated environment probabilities, irreducible natural-environment noise, or establish the unique cause of transfer failure. Preserve the original outcomes as realized labels; do not replace, average, filter or train on these stress outcomes. A later use of randomized targets would require a separately specified estimand and experiment.

There are exactly 384 new branches: 32 states × four draws × three actions. Reuse their 96 original branches from the completed panel without rerunning them. Replay the factual prefix from the first source game through the last selected game, including intervening validation games only to restore exact state and global card counters; no intervention or new label is collected on a validation game. Match all 14 observation arrays and the original full-state fingerprint before cloning. After replacing RNG, require all input bytes unchanged, unchanged global counters and model mode, and identical full-state fingerprint after temporarily restoring the original RNG. The latter also checks that constructing the observation did not mutate other state. Keep factual and baseline snapshots intact.

Follow each assigned first action with the immutable base greedy controller: CPU FP32, batch one, one thread, temperature 1, hand mixture 0.5, Blue deck, stake 1, Ante 8 objective and the existing 2,000-total-step censoring rule. Record complete action traces, input/full-state hashes, counters, log probabilities, immediate outcomes, first blind boundary and terminal outcomes. Censoring stays explicit and never becomes a loss; every selected state/draw remains in the report.

Before launch, project setup and factual replay from the completed 992-state panel's actual setup and per-factual-decision time, and branch time from its four early/later × base/teacher-agreement group means, multiplied by four draws for each selected state. Use the larger of one and its audit/measurement ratio for audit projection. Freeze each phase allowance at twice that projection, with a 6,000-second cap and 30 seconds supervision grace; do not launch if either computed allowance exceeds the cap. No additional timing pilot or automatic budget increase.

Run measurement and independent native audit serially. The auditor independently forwards every policy decision, replays every new action and reconstructs terminal accounting; it also rederives selection and seed hashes. Admit outcomes only after the entire owner actually exits successfully. Check long-running jobs about ten minutes apart. Preserve failed sources and artifacts.

Report by stratum and pooled across this deliberately balanced panel: original versus intervention terminal-label changes; proposal outcomes varying across the four interventions; original mixed win/loss pairs retained, reversed, tied as both losses, tied as both wins or censored; states with any reversal; and immediate score/clear/loss changes alongside terminal changes. Reconcile all denominators independently. Keep original-pair ties separate from reversals. Report finite stress-panel counts without treating draws as independent source games or claiming confidence intervals for the natural environment.

No model is trained, promoted or confirmed. A reversal demonstrates that an exact original terminal preference is not invariant to hidden RNG in the tested intervention. Absence of reversal on four draws does not prove reliability. The result should inform whether further target-distribution work is warranted, while representation and coverage remain competing explanations. The full held-out consequence, retention, autonomous sampled/greedy win-rate, early-policy attribution and final independent-fit requirements stay unchanged. World-model work remains deferred.
