# More distinct demonstrations under the evaluation rules

**Decision: test data diversity at fixed training exposure, with one exploratory fit.** This is a test of an unresolved coverage explanation, not evidence that more data will solve long-horizon generalization. No new model has been trained.

**Question and competing explanation.** Repeated fitting on 1,000 games may reinforce local training comparisons without covering enough related decision contexts to transfer. The strongest alternative is that the current representation/objective does not learn useful comparisons even with broader coverage. Existing [fitting curves](fitting_trajectory_20260924.md), [held-out regression](continuation_transfer_diagnostic_20260925.md) and [preference transfer](ranking_preference_transfer_20260927.md) support testing this distinction. Low original-corpus accuracy alone would not justify scaling.

**Why these data.** The historical 5,000 games use different pack rules, so they cannot supply a clean quantity-only comparison ([audit](original_corpus_feasibility_20260927.md)). Reuse the later 1,000 games and collect 4,000 additional complete teacher games, seeds 4001000–4004999, using the frozen Stage 2 evaluation runtime and unchanged V68 teacher. Keep wins and losses; do not filter, replace or retry seeds. The earlier [full replay](consequence_weighting_preparation_20260924.md) matched all 202,141 existing decisions under that runtime. A new eight-game teacher reproduction matches all 1,746 decisions, all 14 observation fields, rewards, outcomes and Parquet bytes. This sample does not prove universal teacher equivalence or teacher optimality. The known teacher cache issue remains unchanged to keep this a data intervention. A recorded-use scan and review admit the new seeds for training only; confirmation outcomes remain unopened.

**Controlled intervention.** Start from the corrected base a43 and use ordinary unit-weight teacher NLL over the combined 5,000 games. Match existing ordinary control O0: fresh AdamW, learning rate 3e-5, 100-update warmup, zero weight decay, no entropy/KL/ranking loss or clipping, actor and encoder trainable, critic fixed, dropout off, logical batch 320/microbatch 32, RNG seed 0. Run 4,000 updates / 1,280,000 presentations. This budget matches the existing control's compute; it is not a convergence claim. More distinct games necessarily mean fewer repeat presentations per game. Use the same deterministic epoch-shuffle algorithm/namespace, with the new population size. Save 0/1000/2000/4000; 4000 is the fixed candidate endpoint. Intermediate checkpoints are diagnostic, not a post-hoc selection menu. Reuse O0 and base; no second exploratory model.

**Prediction and spending rule.** On the exposed 128-game teacher panel, predict lower teacher NLL than both base and O0, more useful held-out play corrections, and less loss of base-correct choices, including Ante 1–3. First measure the fixed endpoint on all 26,045 teacher states and replay all 6,113 teacher-play states, reusing unchanged control branches. Proceed to autonomous development only if overall NLL is below both controls, net immediate clears exceed O0 and are at least base, and early net clears are at least base. Retain the full correction/retention breakdown, early NLL and immediate-loss counts even if this screen fails. These are spending rules on exposed evidence, not significance claims or the final goal.

If the fixed-state screen passes, reuse the existing 128-seed development panels and controls for sampled, greedy and early-only policy-switch evaluation. Readiness still requires sampled gain at least 5 points over both controls, greedy decline no worse than 5 points against either, and positive early-only effect. A ready candidate needs the full independent confirmation, uncertainty bounds and early-attribution standard in the [workflow](experiment_workflow.md). Better imitation by itself is insufficient.

**Budget and interpretation.** Historical per-game records average 33.22 seconds; the old 17-minute summary covered only a resumed collection stage. Expect roughly 4½–5 hours for 4,000 games on eight CPU workers, capped at eight hours, with no concurrent native experiment. Ordinary O0 fitting took 8,818 seconds: budget about 2½–3 hours, capped at four, plus cache preparation and fixed-state diagnostics before spending on gameplay. About 43 GB of additional observation cache may be needed; 207.5 GB is currently free. Collection does not automatically start fitting: the complete corpus, batching and fit protocol must first be admitted. Stop on missing/incomplete games or mismatches; preserve failures. If broader data reduces training fit but improves held-out decisions, that supports coverage/repetition as a contributor. If it does not, weaken this particular data-scale hypothesis without declaring an architectural limit. If local gains fail in games, inspect student-state or eventual-consequence errors. No coefficient sweep or world-model work follows automatically.

Collection and fitting status will be recorded separately; the long-horizon goal remains unproven.
