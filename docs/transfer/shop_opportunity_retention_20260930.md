# Does the competing action preserve the intended replacement?

The utility diagnostic found that 19/24 missed development sales have no comparable teacher score for the model's alternative. This follow-up measures the local consequence of those alternatives. It is a diagnostic on recorded states, not qualification or autonomous evaluation of the rejected sale-family policy.

Select all 60 teacher-sale states in the admitted eight-game utility panel (28 training, 32 exposed development), without selecting on the new outcomes. Recover the winning Joker replacement plan from the native comparison trace using its existing strict greater-than rule and source order. Check the selected owned Joker and offered item against the reconstructed native state. Reproduce every recorded action and all fourteen observation fields throughout the same eight games.

For every sale state, execute the recorded teacher sale on a separate copy and compare its next observation, complete state fingerprint and UID allocation with the factual replay. Compare the immediately following recorded action with the proposed purchase descriptively; do not require the teacher's next choice to be the same offer. Record whether the teacher's proposed buy is actually legal after its sale, and execute a legal buy on a disposable probe. A failed proposed buy is a finding, not an exclusion.

For all 40 states where the saved model action differs, execute that legal action on a separate copy of the same original state and RNG. This includes the 29 previously unscored and eleven scored alternatives. If the action opens a pack, record the immediate result and resolve only that pack using the frozen search teacher, at most 32 additional actions. Stop as soon as the branch returns to SHOP, leaves the pack, terminates, or reaches the limit; unresolved branches remain explicitly unresolved. Do not follow a model through a game or use outcomes as model inputs.

Track the actual original offered item and original owned Joker by object identity within each branch, preserving shared references when copying. Never substitute a regenerated item with the same name for the original offer. Record native phase/subphase, cash, ordered Joker keys, whether both original objects remain, their current indices, item cost and Joker sell price. Distinguish:

- the original offered item remains in SHOP;
- its purchase is legal immediately;
- the original owned Joker remains legally saleable;
- actually selling that Joker on a disposable copy makes the same offer legal to buy;
- either the immediate purchase or that sale-then-purchase succeeds natively.

These predicates measure local reachability. Buying a replacement after selling an additional Joker need not preserve the original portfolio; show the other owned-Joker removals separately. A pack can alter cash, cards or Jokers, and a voucher can change prices or capacity. Use actual simulator transitions rather than subtracting printed prices. A vanished offer or lost affordability does not prove that the alternative was worse; it may have produced another benefit. Conversely, a reachable replacement does not prove the learned policy eventually executes it.

Each branch starts from a fresh state copy and the same UID allocator state. Teacher queries operate on separate copies and restore allocation afterward. Restore the factual allocator after all branches; verify factual state, observation and Python/NumPy/Torch CPU RNG unchanged. Probe copies use their branch's post-action RNG, and leave the parent branch unchanged. Record raw actions and transition fingerprints; retain exact Joker/card order.

Admit results only after actual whole-owner success and an independent saved-record audit of all identities, branch accounting, predicates and factual controls. Preserve failed runs and sources.
